Finding the source of basement water

Timing and location tell you most of what you need to know.

  1. 1

    Note when it appears

    Water after rain or snowmelt suggests drainage or groundwater. Water at any time suggests a leak.

  2. 2

    Check the meter

    Shut off all water in the house. If the meter dial still moves, a supply line is leaking.

  3. 3

    Look at the drains

    Water bubbling up at a floor drain after heavy use points to the sewer line.

  4. 4

    Look at the outside

    Gutters, downspouts and soil that tilts toward the foundation send water to the wall.

A leak that runs even in dry weather may be a plumbing issue, and our leak detection service can find it without tearing out walls.

Plumbing fixes that keep a basement drier

These are the parts of waterproofing that a licensed plumber actually handles.

Sump pit and pump

A pit that collects groundwater and a pump that sends it outside. A check valve stops water running back in.

Discharge line

The pipe that carries pump water away. It should end well away from the foundation and not tie into the sewer.

Window well drains

A drain at the bottom of each well, tied to the sump or to daylight, stops wells from filling like buckets.

Backwater valve

Where permitted, a valve in the sewer line stops sewage from rising through floor drains.

Floor drain and trap

A dry trap lets sewer gas in. A clogged drain overflows. Both are simple to fix.

Grading and gutters: what you can do first

Much basement water starts above ground. These cost little and often help.

Quick wins at home

  • Extend downspouts several feet from the wall
  • Clean gutters before spring runoff
  • Add soil so it slopes away from the house
  • Keep window wells clear of debris

When to bring in a contractor

  • Cracks leaking in the foundation wall
  • Water at the floor and wall joint
  • A bowed or leaning wall
  • Repeated flooding after fixing drainage

Stormwater and sandbags in Midvale

Midvale City says residents are responsible for protecting their own property in floods. If a storm is coming, Salt Lake County gives out free sandbags at the Flood Control Sandbag Shed, 604 West 6960 South in Midvale, open seven days a week from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Bring a shovel.

Sandbags help against surface water at a door or window well, but they do nothing for groundwater that rises through the floor. Report spills or illegal discharge into a storm drain to Midvale Public Works at 801-567-7235. If a sump pump is pumping oil or chemicals, stop and call.
