DIY Water Softener Installation in Midvale, UT: What You Can Do and What to Hand Off

Yes, a handy homeowner can install a water softener. The job is a few pipe connections, a drain line, an outlet and a bag of salt. The parts that go wrong are the ones people skip, such as the bypass valve, the drain air gap and the test that tells you how much softener you need.

Below is the whole job in order, a sizing example you can follow with a calculator, and a straight list of where a licensed plumber should take over.

The short answer

Can you install a water softener yourself?

In most homes, yes, if the pipes near the softener are accessible and you are comfortable cutting and joining them. Many modern softeners ship with push-fit connectors, so no soldering is needed on PEX or copper. A basic install takes a few hours the first time.

The honest limits are plumbing skill, local rules and your own water. If you have never cut a pipe, the cost of a mistake is a flooded garage. If you do not know your water hardness, you may buy the wrong size.

The installation in five steps

Follow the maker's manual for your unit. These steps show the order that works for most salt-based softeners.

  1. 1

    Test the water

    Measure hardness at the tap so you can size the unit correctly.

  2. 2

    Pick the spot

    Place it near the main line, a drain, a power outlet and level floor.

  3. 3

    Shut off and cut in

    Close the main valve, drain the lines, then cut the pipe and fit the bypass.

  4. 4

    Run the drain line

    Leave an air gap of at least 1 inch above the drain so wastewater cannot siphon back.

  5. 5

    Start up and set

    Fill with salt, program hardness, and run a manual regeneration to test the seals.

Keep the cold-water line to outdoor hose bibs on the unsoftened side. Softened water is wasted on lawns and can add salt to the soil.

Sizing a softener without guessing

Sizing uses two numbers, your daily water use and your hardness in grains per gallon. One grain per gallon equals 17.1 mg/L, so lab reports in mg/L convert with simple division.

This is an example, not your number. Use your own household count and tap test.
StepExample mathResult
Estimate daily use for 4 people4 people x about 80 gallons320 gallons per day
Convert hardness to grains182.4 mg/L divided by 17.1About 10.7 grains per gallon
Grains removed per day320 x 10.7About 3,400 grains
Grains between regenerations3,400 x 7 daysAbout 24,000 grains

A 24,000 grain weekly load is the capacity you would want a unit to carry at a time. A larger tank regenerates less often, and a smaller tank regenerates more often and uses more salt.

Tools and parts to have before you shut off the water

Missing one fitting means a second trip with the main valve closed. Gather everything first.

  • Water hardness test strips or a lab test result
  • Pipe cutter that suits your pipe, plus deburring tool
  • Three-valve bypass or the bypass built into the unit
  • Push-fit or compression fittings matched to your pipe size, often 3/4 inch
  • Drain hose, clamps and a way to keep the 1 inch air gap
  • Bucket and towels, since lines hold water even after draining
  • Salt pellets sized for your unit and a level to set the tank
  • A grounded outlet within reach of the power cord

Do it yourself or hire a plumber?

The cost of a small mistake is a leak or a water heater that gets hard water by accident.

Reasonable DIY

  • PEX or copper with easy access
  • A plain basement or garage wall
  • You own a pipe cutter and have used push-fit fittings
  • No changes to the drain or electrical needed

Better with a plumber

  • Old galvanized or corroded pipe near the main
  • Soldering copper in a tight space
  • No nearby drain or outlet
  • You are not sure where the hot water branches off
  • You want the work checked against local rules

Ask Midvale Building and Safety whether your install needs a permit or inspection. The city pages name water heater replacement as permitted work and do not mention softeners, so ask before you cut.

Mistakes that cause callbacks

Most failed DIY installs come down to a short list.

Softening the hot and cold outdoor lines

Outdoor taps should stay on hard water. Add the connection after the hose bib branch.

No air gap on the drain

Without a gap, drain water can siphon back into the brine tank. Leave at least 1 inch.

Wrong bypass setup

Without a bypass, a single repair means no water in the house until the softener is fixed.

Skipping the regeneration test

A manual cycle on day one shows leaks while you are standing there.

When to call a pro

Call a licensed plumber if the main valve will not shut off all the way, if the pipes are corroded or galvanized, or if you cannot find a drain within reach of the hose.
