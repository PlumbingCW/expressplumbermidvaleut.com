Replacing a Tank Water Heater With a Tankless in Midvale, UT: What Changes

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so it never runs out in the middle of a long shower. It also needs a bigger gas line or more electrical power than the tank it replaces. That is where most swaps get complicated.

This guide covers the checks that decide whether a swap is simple or a bigger project, with a table to help you decide and a plain look at venting and gas.

The question first

Can you swap a tank water heater for a tankless?

Yes, in most homes, but not as a one-for-one swap. A tank heater needs a small gas burner or a single 240 volt circuit. A whole-house tankless gas unit burns far more fuel at once, often in the range of 150,000 to 200,000 BTU, compared with roughly 30,000 to 50,000 for a typical tank.

That jump is why the gas line, the vent and the water supply all get a second look. The question is not whether tankless works, but what your house needs to run one safely.

Tank versus tankless: a decision table

Use this to see which kind of home fits which heater. There is no winner for every house.

A tankless unit saves standby heat loss but does not make unlimited hot water at any flow rate.

Gas and venting

Gas line, vent and power: the three checks

These decide the scope of the job before any unit is ordered.

Gas line size

A big gas tankless often needs a 3/4 inch line close to the unit, and the meter must be able to supply the added load. A plumber checks this first.

Vent material and route

Tank heaters use a draft hood. Many tankless units are sealed and use special vent pipe through a wall or roof. The vent route can be the hardest part of the job.

Condensate drain

High efficiency units make acidic water that needs a drain and sometimes a neutralizer. Plan where it will go.

Electric power

An electric tankless can need several 40 to 60 amp circuits. Your panel may not have room, which can make electric a poor fit.

If you smell gas during a swap

Natural gas is odorized with a sulfur or rotten egg smell. Any gas work, including capping the old line, is a job for a licensed plumber and not a weekend project.

If you smell gas at any point:

  • Stop work and leave the building immediately, leaving doors open if safe
  • From a safe place, call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911
  • Do not use phones, light switches or anything electric inside
  • Do not search for or try to fix the leak yourself
Midvale note

Permits and inspections for a water heater swap in Midvale

Midvale's building page says water heater replacement requires a building permit and building inspections. That includes swapping a tank for a tankless. Permits are submitted online, and inspection requests are online at least 24 hours ahead, Monday to Thursday 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. Only the contractor on record can schedule the inspection.

The practical effect is timing. Inspections need 24 hours of notice and run only Monday to Thursday, so a tankless install with a new vent and gas run should be planned around that window. Midvale Building and Safety is at City Hall, 7505 S Holden Street, and the office is closed Friday to Sunday. Ask them about the rules for gas and vent work on your house.

Each house differs, but this is the common order of work.

DIY or licensed plumber for a tankless swap

Gas and venting are the reasons this is rarely a homeowner job.

What a homeowner can reasonably do

  • Compare models by flow rate and fuel type
  • Measure the wall space and note the gas line size
  • Clear the area and shut off water at the main
  • Count how many fixtures run hot water at once

What needs a licensed plumber

  • Running or upsizing the gas line
  • Cutting and sealing the vent
  • Pulling the permit and scheduling inspections
  • Connecting the condensate drain
  • Testing combustion and leaks

We are plumbers, not electricians. If an electric unit needs a panel upgrade, a licensed electrician handles that part, and we can coordinate the plumbing.

Mineral scale and long-term care

Tankless heaters have narrow heat exchanger passages that scale up with hard water. A yearly flush with a descaling solution is the usual advice, and a softener upstream helps. If you are not sure about your water, test your tap or ask your utility, since the supply differs across Midvale.

A scaled unit shows up as lukewarm water, error codes or noise. A flush is a routine service, and our tankless water heater page explains what we do.

When to call a pro

Call a plumber before you buy a unit. A visit to check the gas line and vent can show whether tankless suits your house or whether a new tank is the smarter choice. If your tank is already leaking, shut off the cold supply and call, because a flood is faster than a swap.
