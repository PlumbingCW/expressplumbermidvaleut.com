What Drives Tankless Water Heater Installation Cost in Midvale

A tankless water heater usually costs more to install than a tank replacement, and the gap is rarely about the unit itself. It comes from the gas line, the vent, the drain for condensate, the permit and the work around the old tank.

This guide walks through each cost factor in plain terms so you can read a bid and ask better questions. We do not post a flat price here, because a flat price for a job like this would be a guess. Our approach is a clear price before work starts, after we look at your setup.

The short answer

Why one tankless install costs far more than another

Two houses on the same block can get very different bids for the same model. One has a gas line that already feeds the old heater with room to spare, and the other has a thin line that must be upsized. One has an exterior wall right behind the heater, and the other needs a vent run through two floors.

So the honest answer to the cost question is a list of variables, not a number. Gas lines, vent routing, electrical needs, water quality, permit work and the condition of the old plumbing each add or remove hours of labor and parts.

A good bid names each of those items. If a quote is one lump sum with no detail, ask what it assumes about the gas line and the vent before you compare it with another.

Cost factors

Six things that move the price

These show up on almost every tankless job we look at.

Gas line size

Many tankless units draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU per hour, several times what a 40-gallon tank burner uses. A line that was sized for the tank, often 1/2 inch, may need to be replaced with 3/4 inch pipe.

Venting

Condensing units vent through 2-inch or 3-inch plastic pipe. Older style units need a larger stainless vent. The route and the length decide how many hours it takes.

Condensate drain

A condensing unit produces acidic water that needs a drain and often a neutralizer. A floor drain nearby is easy. A long run to a laundry standpipe is not.

Electrical

Gas units still need a 120-volt outlet for the fan and controls. Electric tankless units need heavy dedicated circuits, which can mean a panel upgrade.

Existing plumbing

Corroded shutoffs, old galvanized nipples or crowded copper make a clean hookup harder. Swapping them while the wall is open is cheaper than doing it later.

Water quality

Hard water scales the heat exchanger. A softener or a scheduled flush may belong in the plan, which adds to the first bill and saves repair calls later.

Tank swap or tankless: where the money goes

The upfront gap and the long-term picture point in different directions.

Replacing with another tank

  • Uses the existing gas line and vent in most homes
  • Takes about half a day for a standard swap
  • Lower upfront labor and parts
  • Tank life is commonly cited at 10 to 15 years

Installing tankless

  • May need a new gas line, new vent and drain
  • Takes longer and touches more of the house
  • Higher upfront labor and parts
  • Unit life is commonly cited at 20 years with regular flushing

Where tankless saves and where it does not

A tankless unit heats water only when you open a tap, so it avoids the standby heat that a 40- or 50-gallon tank loses all day. That can lower the gas bill in a household that uses modest amounts of hot water. It will not do much for a family running three showers and a dishwasher at once, because the unit has a flow limit.

Most showers run 2.0 to 2.5 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet around 1.5. Add them up before you pick a size. If your peak demand is 6 gallons per minute and the unit tops out near 5 at your winter water temperature, someone will get a cold surprise.

Incoming water is colder in January than in August, so the unit has to add more heat. A model that looks fine on paper in summer can fall short in winter. Ask the installer to size for your coldest month, not your average.

Midvale permit

Midvale requires a permit and inspection for water heater replacement

The Midvale City Building and Safety page lists water heater replacement among the work that requires a building permit and building inspections. That includes a tankless swap, and it applies even when the old and new units sit in the same spot.

Permits are submitted online, and inspection requests are made online at least 24 hours ahead. Only the contractor on record can schedule the inspection, so the licensed plumber who pulls the permit is the one who books it. Building and Safety is at City Hall, 7505 S Holden Street, and keeps Monday to Thursday hours.

Practically, that means you should expect the job to include a permit line and an inspection step. Skipping it can cause trouble at resale and with insurance if a gas appliance is involved. For any rule beyond that, such as clearances or vent requirements, ask Midvale Building and Safety directly.

Hard water and the long-term cost

The USGS classes water above 180 mg/L as very hard. JVWCD reported a 2025 average of 182.4 mg/L, which sits right at that line, with readings that ranged from 16.0 up to 381. Scale on a tankless heat exchanger cuts flow and efficiency, and in bad cases it trips the unit's error codes.

A yearly flush through the service valves is the usual fix. If you want to cut scale at the source, a water softener is a separate project with its own cost, and some owners price it together with the heater. Test your tap or read your utility report before deciding.

When to call a pro, and what to have ready

Gas and venting work is not a weekend DIY job. It belongs with licensed plumbers, and a permit goes with it. Before the visit, gather these:

  • The make and model plate on your current heater, plus its location
  • Whether the heater runs on gas or electricity
  • Photos of the gas meter, the gas line and any nearby vent
  • How many people live in the home and when hot water use peaks
  • Any history of scale, cloudy hot water or low flow

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building right away and call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 from outside. Do not use switches or phones inside. Then call us once the building is safe.
