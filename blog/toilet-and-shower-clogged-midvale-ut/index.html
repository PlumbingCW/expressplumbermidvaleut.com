Toilet and Shower Clogged at the Same Time in Midvale, UT: What It Means

A toilet that will not flush and a shower that will not drain feel like two bad luck events. They are almost never two. Bathroom fixtures share the same lower drain, so a blockage that holds back one usually holds back the other.

This guide shows how to confirm it is a shared line, what to do in the next ten minutes, and when the problem sits outside your property.

When a clog in the toilet and shower is an emergency

Two fixtures failing together is a strong clue that the main drain is blocked. Water has fewer exits, and it will find the lowest one in the house.

Treat it as urgent if you see any of these:

  • Water or sewage rising in the shower or tub when you flush
  • Gurgling from the shower when the toilet drains
  • A floor drain or basement fixture bubbling or overflowing
  • A sewer smell that gets stronger as water backs up
Why it happens

Why a toilet and shower clog together

In most homes the toilet, the tub or shower and the bathroom sink each have a small branch drain. Those branches join the main drain line, which carries everything to the street. The toilet drains through a 3 to 4 inch pipe, and a shower through a 2 inch pipe, but they meet low in the house.

A blockage in that shared run backs water into the lowest, most open fixture. That is usually the tub or shower, because its drain sits closest to the floor. A flush then pushes water into the tub instead of down the pipe.

What to do in the next ten minutes

Small steps now can limit the mess and help whoever comes next.

  1. 1

    Stop all water use

    No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers until the line is clear.

  2. 2

    Try one plunge

    Plunge the toilet once with a flange plunger. If other drains react, the clog is shared.

  3. 3

    Check a lower fixture

    Look in the basement or at the lowest floor drain for rising water.

  4. 4

    Find the cleanout

    Many homes have a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement. Do not open it yourself if water is high.

  5. 5

    Call for help

    Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are involved and what you did.

Causes

What commonly blocks the shared line

The cause shapes the fix, so it is worth naming.

Tree roots

Roots enter through joints in older clay or cast iron pipe and build a net that catches paper. They return unless the pipe is repaired.

Flushed wipes and paper products

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down. They snag on rough spots and collect everything behind them.

Grease from the kitchen

Fat cools in the main and narrows the line, even if the kitchen sink is far from the bathroom.

A sagging pipe section

A low spot holds water and solids. A camera shows this as a belly, and cleaning only buys time.

Midvale note

Which sewer district serves your street, and who owns the pipe

Four sewer districts serve Midvale: Midvale City Public Works, Cottonwood Improvement District, Midvalley Improvement District and Sandy Suburban Improvement District. Midvale City Public Works collects wastewater for about one-third of the city. Which one handles the street main depends on where you live, so check your address before you call anyone.

The piece that runs from your house to the main is the lateral. The city says lateral maintenance and ownership typically falls to the property owner, not the municipality. That means a blockage in the lateral is yours to clear, and a blockage in the main is the district's. A cleanout with water standing in it and a fully backed up home suggests the main, which is worth reporting.

Homeowner fix or professional cleaning

A plunger helps with one fixture. It does not reach a shared line.

What you can try

  • Plunge the toilet once with a flange plunger
  • Use a drain tool on the shower trap for hair
  • Avoid running water during the attempt
  • Skip chemical cleaners on a full drain

What needs a plumber

  • Two or more fixtures clogged together
  • Water coming up in a tub when you flush
  • A cleanout that is full
  • Repeat clogs after you cleared them
  • A cable or camera to find the cause

Chemical cleaner in a full drain just sits there and can splash back on skin when a plumber opens the line. Hot water and patience are safer while you wait.
