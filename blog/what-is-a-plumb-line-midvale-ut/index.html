What Is a Plumb Line, and Why Plumbers Still Care About It

A plumb line is a string with a weight on the end that hangs straight down under gravity. The string shows true vertical, and you compare a wall, post or pipe to it to see if it leans.

That is the whole tool, and it has been used for thousands of years. Below we explain how to use one, how it differs from a level, and how the idea ties into the drain stacks, fixtures and permitted remodels that plumbers deal with in Midvale.

The direct answer

A plumb line in plain terms

A plumb line is a cord with a pointed weight, called a plumb bob, tied to one end. You hold the other end still, let the weight settle and the string points straight at the center of the earth. That gives you an exact vertical line that no floor or wall can fool.

The word plumb comes from the Latin word for lead, which is what the weights were once made of. Plumber comes from the same root, because lead pipe was the trade's early material. The connection is more than trivia, since plumbers still check vertical runs of pipe against a plumb reference.

Today the weight is usually steel or brass. A bob weighing 8 to 16 ounces settles quickly and resists drafts.

Plumb, level and square are three different checks

People mix these up. Each one measures a different direction.

Plumb and level are always 90 degrees apart.

A spirit level has vials for both. The vial at the end reads plumb, the long vial in the middle reads level.

How to use a plumb line

It takes about two minutes once you have the string and the bob.

  1. 1

    Fix the string at the top

    Pin it at the top of the wall or post, or hold it against the surface. Let the weight hang free.

  2. 2

    Let it settle

    Wait for the bob to stop swinging. Steady it in a bucket of water if there is a draft.

  3. 3

    Measure the gap

    Measure from the string to the surface at the top and the bottom. Equal gaps mean the wall is plumb.

  4. 4

    Mark and compare

    A difference of 1/4 inch or more over 8 feet means the wall leans and fixtures against it may not sit right.

Where plumb matters in plumbing

Drain stacks are the big one. A vertical stack carries wastewater straight down from upper floors to the building drain, and a stack that is out of plumb can create low spots where debris collects. The same goes for vent pipes that run up through the roof.

Fixtures also need to be true. A toilet that leans on a flange out of plumb can rock and loosen the wax seal. A shower valve set crooked puts the handle and trim off center, and a tub or vanity that is out of level lets water pool.

Horizontal drains are different. They are meant to slope, commonly about 1/4 inch per foot for smaller pipe, so water and waste keep moving. The exact slope rules for your project are a question for Midvale Building and Safety.

Fixtures

Plumb and level checks around the house

A few quick checks catch problems before they become leaks.

Toilet flange

The flange should sit level and flush with the finished floor. A tilted flange breaks the wax seal and leaks at the base.

Vanity and sink

Check the countertop with a 2-foot level in both directions. A slight tilt toward the drain is fine, but a tilt toward the wall is not.

Shower valve

Set the valve body plumb in the wall before the cement board goes up. After tile is on, it cannot be fixed without opening the wall.

Vertical supply risers

Pipe straps hold vertical lines in place. A line that bows can rattle and wear against framing.

Midvale permits

Remodels and plumbing work in Midvale need a permit

Midvale Building and Safety states that new construction, additions, basement finishes, remodeling and water heater replacement require a building permit and building inspections. Permits are submitted online, and inspection requests go in online at least 24 hours ahead, Monday to Thursday.

Only the contractor on record can schedule an inspection. That means a plumber you hire for a bathroom remodel pulls the permit and books the inspector, and ask Building and Safety which inspection stages apply. Whatever the stages, a drain stack or shower valve that is out of plumb is much easier to fix before drywall goes up than after.

Building and Safety is on the second floor of City Hall at 7505 S Holden Street, phone 801-567-7200 extension 1016, closed Friday through Sunday. For any question about which of your changes needs a permit, ask them directly. They know what the city requires.

Plumb bob or a laser level

Both work. The right one depends on the job.

Plumb bob

  • Costs very little
  • Needs no batteries
  • Works outside in light wind with a steady hand
  • Slow over long heights

Laser or long level

  • Fast across a whole room
  • Projects a line on several surfaces
  • Needs power and clear sight lines
  • Harder to read in bright sunlight

When to call a pro

A plumb line is a good way to check your own work or a finished wall. It is not a substitute for a licensed plumber on a stack, a vent or a gas line. If a drain slows after a remodel, a sewer inspection camera can show whether a section is sagging, sloped the wrong way or pitched too flat.
