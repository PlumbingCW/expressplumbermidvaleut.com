First ten minutes

Shutoff steps when a pipe bursts

These steps limit the damage before help arrives. Do them in this order.

  1. 1

    Find the main

    Look near the water heater, in the basement, or where the line enters the house.

  2. 2

    Close it fully

    Turn a round handle clockwise. A lever valve closes with a quarter turn.

  3. 3

    Drain the lines

    Open the lowest faucet in the house and a high one to release pressure.

  4. 4

    Cut the power

    Switch off breakers to wet rooms. Do not wade through water near outlets.

  5. 5

    Photograph and mop up

    Take photos for your insurer, then move belongings and remove standing water.

If you cannot find the main shutoff or it will not turn, call us and say so. A seized valve changes how we plan the visit.

When a broken pipe is an emergency

Not every pipe problem floods a house, but these do the most damage when ignored:

  • Water spraying from a wall, ceiling or crawlspace
  • A cracked or split pipe you can see that will not stop leaking when you close the fixture valve
  • Ceiling drywall that is sagging or bulging with water
  • A main shutoff that will not close
  • Any water reaching an electrical panel or outlet
Who to call

Inside the house or at the meter? Who handles what

Midvale Public Works runs a 24/7 water emergency line at 801-567-7235, and that line is for problems on the city's side: a leaking main in the street or a failure at the meter. A burst pipe inside your home is not theirs to fix.

If your own shutoff closes the water and the leak stops, the break is on your side. If water keeps pouring toward the street, call the city. When you are not sure, call us and we will tell you which side it is.

Frozen pipes

Frozen pipe: what to do before it bursts

Ice itself does not usually break a pipe. Pressure builds between the ice blockage and the closed faucet, and the pipe splits where the pressure peaks.

Warning signs

Frost on an exposed pipe, a faucet that trickles or gives nothing, and a cold spot on a wall in a hard freeze.

Safe thawing

Open the faucet, then warm the pipe with a hair dryer or heating pad, starting near the faucet and moving toward the frozen section.

Never do this

Do not use an open flame, a propane torch or a heat gun on a pipe. They can start a fire or burst the pipe.

If it already split

A frozen pipe often only shows a leak after it thaws. Close the main and call so water does not pour out when the ice melts.

Cracked and broken pipe: repair or replace

A split in a straight run of copper can be cut out and replaced with a new section. A pinhole in a pipe that has been pitting from the inside is a different story, because the rest of the line is likely thinning too.

Galvanized steel behaves the same way. It corrodes from the inside, and a repair at one joint often leads to a leak at the next one. In those cases we will talk through whether repiping the affected run makes more sense than patching it again.

Water damage

After the leak stops: what to do with the water

Wet drywall and carpet start growing mold in 24 to 48 hours, so drying matters as much as the repair. Open windows, run fans, lift soaked rugs and call your insurance company before you tear anything out.

Do not assume the visible hole is the only damage. A pipe that burst inside a wall can soak insulation and baseboards you cannot see, which is one reason we recommend a leak detection check if the source is not obvious.

Temporary fix or permanent repair?

Repair clamps and push-fit couplings exist for good reasons, and they have limits.

Temporary patch

  • Clamps and tape buy a few hours
  • Fine to stop a spray until help arrives
  • Not suited to pipe that is corroded or flaking
  • Can fail under full pressure

Licensed plumber's repair

  • Cuts back to sound pipe
  • Uses properly rated fittings
  • Pressure tests before closing the wall
  • Looks for the reason it burst
