When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Some problems can wait until tomorrow. These ones cannot, because they shut a business down or damage a building:

  • Sewage or gray water coming out of a floor drain or restroom
  • A burst or leaking supply line that is flooding a floor or a neighbor's unit
  • A commercial drain or grate that has failed and needs to be opened right away
  • No water at the building, or a main valve that will not close
  • A grease line backing up into a kitchen during service
After hours

Plumbing help when the doors are locked

Many commercial plumbing failures happen overnight or on a weekend, when nobody is in the building to notice. The first people to find them are often staff arriving to a wet floor.

When you call, tell us the address, the type of space, who has keys and which fixture is the source. If you have a property manager or landlord, check who owns the pipe, because in leased space the answer decides who authorizes the work and who pays.

Backflow

Backflow testing and cross-connection rules

A backflow device keeps water from flowing backward into the public supply. Irrigation systems, fire lines and some commercial equipment need one, and the assembly is typically tested on a schedule set by the water provider.

Midvale runs a cross-connection program. The administrator is at 801-567-7287, and that is the right first call to ask what your property needs, how often the test is due and who the city accepts for the test. Ask the city before booking anything. Missing a required test can lead a water provider to lock valves, which is a hard way to find out a deadline passed.

Grease

Grease traps and kitchen drain lines

A restaurant sends fat, oil and food scraps down its drains all day. A grease trap catches most of it before it reaches the sewer, but it has to be emptied or the fat moves on and blocks the line.

We clean and service small grease traps and clear the drain lines around them. Large outdoor grease interceptors are pumped by hauling companies with vacuum trucks, and we will tell you if yours needs one. How often a trap needs cleaning depends on the kitchen, and the city or the sewer district for your address may set a schedule, so ask them.

Leaks

Commercial leak detection, with honest limits

A leak in a commercial building is harder to trace than one in a house, because pipes run through walls, ceilings and shared chases. We can often find a leak in a small space with a meter check and a visual search of the usual spots, but we will say plainly when a job is beyond what we can do cleanly.

In multi-tenant buildings, the owner often has to approve access to shared systems. See leak detection for how we trace a hidden leak, and expect us to tell you if the source sits in a part of the building we cannot work on.

Storm drains

A note on storm drains and spills

Storm drains are meant for rain runoff, not for treatment, so wash water, grease and chemicals do not belong in them. If you see a spill or illegal discharge into a storm drain, report it to Midvale Public Works at 801-567-7235.

Floor drains inside a building usually connect to the sanitary sewer, not the storm system. If you are not sure which one a drain feeds, tell us and we will check before we clear it.
