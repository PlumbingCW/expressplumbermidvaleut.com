When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most slow drains can wait a day. Some cannot, and the difference is whether water has anywhere left to go.

Treat it as an emergency and call right away if you see any of these signs:

  • Sewage or gray water coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain
  • Two or more fixtures clogged at once, such as a toilet and the shower together
  • Gurgling from a toilet when the washer drains
  • Water pooling near the cleanout or a wet spot in the yard over the sewer line
What to do first

Steps to take while you wait for us

Stop using every sink, toilet, shower and appliance that drains. Each flush or load of laundry adds gallons to a line that is already full, and the water will find the lowest opening in the house.

If water is rising, find the main water shutoff and turn it clockwise until it stops. Keep people away from floor drains and basement bathrooms until the line is clear, because sewer backups carry bacteria.

How we clear a clogged drain

The method depends on where the blockage sits. A sink trap and a 4-inch main line need very different tools.

  1. 1

    Find the blockage

    We ask which fixtures are slow, then test them. One slow sink points to a branch line, and several point to the main.

  2. 2

    Choose the tool

    A hand auger handles a tub or sink. A powered cable reaches 50 to 100 feet into a main line.

  3. 3

    Clear and flush

    We run the cable until the line moves freely, then flush it with water to confirm it drains.

  4. 4

    Look for the cause

    If grease, roots or a broken pipe caused it, we say so and explain your options before any extra work.

Large buildups of grease or mineral scale sometimes need more than a cable. In those cases we may recommend hydro jetting, which uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe wall.

Midvale causes

Why drains clog in Midvale homes

Four causes account for most of the calls we hear.

Grease and food in kitchen lines

Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and narrows it a little more with every wash. A 1.5-inch kitchen drain can close up in a year or two.

Hair and soap in bathrooms

Hair knots around soap scum in tub and shower traps. It starts as a slow drain and ends as standing water.

Roots in older lateral lines

Homes near Old Town sit on some of the oldest streets in the city, and older drain pipe has more joints for roots to enter. A camera shows what your line is made of.

Wipes and paper products

Flushable wipes do not break down like toilet paper. They collect at bends and catch everything behind them.

Chemical cleaner or a licensed plumber?

Store-bought cleaner is tempting at midnight. Here is how it compares for a full drain.

Chemical cleaner

  • Works only on thin, partial clogs

  • Does not remove roots or solid objects
  • Can damage older pipe over time

Licensed plumber

Related drain and sewer services

If a cable clears the clog but it returns within weeks, a sewer inspection with a camera can show whether roots or a sagging section is the real problem. A repeat clog in the main line is also a good reason to ask about sewer line cleaning.

If the camera finds a cracked or collapsed pipe, cleaning will not hold. That is when sewer line repair becomes the better plan, and we will explain the choice before you decide.

Where this guidance comes from

Drain work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets pipe sizes and venting. Utah licensing guidelines apply to anyone who opens a sewer line for pay.
