When a valve will not shut off, it is an emergency

A shutoff valve is the one part you rely on to stop the water. When it fails, the only way to stop the flow is the main valve for the whole house, and that valve may be old too.

Treat these as emergencies:

  • A faucet, tub spout or shower that keeps running with the handle off
  • A wall or sink shutoff valve that will not turn, or turns and does nothing
  • Water spraying or running from a supply line or the stem of a valve
  • A hose bib or outdoor faucet that burst or is leaking inside the wall
  • A broken main valve or a valve that has snapped off in your hand

Stopping the flow

What to try when the valve will not hold

These work in order. Stop at the first one that stops the water.

  1. 1

    Local shutoff

    Turn the valve under the sink or behind the fixture clockwise, slowly and without forcing it.

  2. 2

    Main house valve

    Find it near the water heater, in the basement or where the line enters. Turn it clockwise.

  3. 3

    Open a low tap

    Once the main is closed, open the lowest faucet to drain pressure from the pipes.

  4. 4

    City on-call line

    If the main valve is stuck, call Midvale Public Works at 801-567-7235 and press 0.

Do not use a wrench on a stuck valve hard enough to bend the stem. A snapped stem turns a slow leak into a flood, and replacing the valve is a plumber's job.

What fails

Common faucet and valve problems

Different fixtures fail in different ways. Here is how they usually show up.

Kitchen and bathroom sink faucets

Drips from the spout come from worn washers, seats or cartridges. Water under the sink often comes from the supply lines or the base seals.

Tub and shower faucets

A tub spout that runs after the handle is shut means a worn cartridge or valve. Water staining a ceiling below is a sign the leak is behind the wall.

Shower valve and showerhead

Leaks from the handle point to the cartridge. A leaking showerhead arm may only need new tape or a new connector.

Shutoff and supply valves

Old multi-turn valves seize from mineral buildup. Turning one that has not moved in years can crack it, so go slowly.

Midvale specifics

Hard water and faucet wear

Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District reports a 2025 average total hardness of 182.4 mg/L, a level USGS classes as very hard. That is the water supplied to the east side of Midvale. Hard water leaves scale on aerators and inside cartridges, so east-side faucets often show low flow and slow drips sooner than they otherwise would.

Unscrew the aerator at the tip of the spout and soak it in white vinegar to clear scale. If the flow is still weak, the cartridge is the next suspect. For west-side addresses on city wells, ask Midvale Public Utilities for the water quality report.

Outdoor faucets

Hose bibs and winter freezes

Midvale sits at about 4,383 feet, and winter nights fall below freezing often enough to split pipe. A hose left attached through a cold snap traps water in the bib and the pipe behind the wall, and the split shows up when the weather warms.

Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze and shut the indoor valve that feeds the outdoor spigot if you have one. If you find a wet spot inside the wall on the first warm day, call. A freeze-split pipe is a burst pipe repair job, not just a faucet job.

Repair the faucet or replace it?

Both options work. The right one depends on age, parts and how the finish looks.

Repair

  • Fixture is newer and the finish is good
  • Parts are easy to find
  • Leak is from one worn part
  • You want to keep the style

Replace

  • Faucet is old and parts are hard to get
  • More than one part is worn
  • Finish is pitted or the body is corroded
  • You want a different style or spout height

Before you call about a faucet

Having these answers ready speeds up the visit.

  1. Which fixture is leaking, and is it the spout, the handle or the base
  2. Whether the shutoff valve underneath turns and stops the water
  3. Roughly how old the faucet is, and whether any brand name is visible
  4. Whether water has reached a cabinet, ceiling or floor
  5. A photo of the faucet or the leak, if you can take one

If you are not sure, call anyway. We will walk through it with you on the phone.
