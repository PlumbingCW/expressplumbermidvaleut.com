The area

What Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs are

Bingham Junction is a redevelopment district in the west part of Midvale, next to a light rail station. It sits on land that once belonged to the smelting industry, and it now holds mostly apartments, townhomes and some commercial space.

Jordan Bluffs is a neighboring development in the same part of the city. We treat both names together because the buildings are of a similar age and layout. Exact street edges are not published by the city, so confirm your address with Midvale City when a permit or utility question depends on it.

Compare this with Old Town and Main Street, where buildings are older and far more varied. Here the common issue is not pipe age. It is shared systems and who owns what.

Building types

Plumbing setups you meet in newer multi-unit areas

The first step depends on whether you own, rent or manage, and on which part of the building is leaking.

Apartments

A renter reports a leak to management in writing. Management decides who comes in, so give the unit number and where the water shows.

Townhomes

Each unit may have its own water heater and shutoff, while the sewer line and parts of the supply may be shared. Check your association documents.

Condominiums

Owners often hold the fixtures and branch lines, while the association holds risers and main drains. The governing documents say where the line is drawn.

Ground-floor shops

Retail and food spaces below housing add floor drains and restroom lines. Tell us which space is affected and who holds the keys.

Who to call

Call management first or call a plumber?

In a shared building the order of calls matters, because access and approval often sit with someone else.

Call management first

  • Water from a ceiling in a rented unit
  • A shared drain backing up in several units
  • A leak in a hallway or utility room
  • Anything involving a common shutoff

A plumber can help directly with

  • A leak under your own sink you own
  • A water heater in your unit
  • A toilet or faucet that is yours to repair
  • A second opinion for a manager or owner

In both cases, shut off the nearest valve if you can reach it safely and take photos before anything is moved.

Water source and hardness on the west side

Midvale has more than one water supply. The east side is served with water from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, which reported 182.4 mg/L total hardness in its 2025 report. West-side homes run on city wells, and we could not find a published hardness figure for them.

So we do not state a hardness number for Bingham Junction. If scale matters to you, ask Midvale Public Utilities for the annual water quality report for your address, and read our water softener installation page before choosing equipment.

Checklist when a leak may reach a neighbor

Use this list when water shows in a shared building. It takes a few minutes and protects everyone involved.

  • Find the nearest shutoff and close it if it is safe to reach
  • Photograph the damage, the source and the date
  • Tell the property manager or association in writing
  • Move belongings away from wet floors and walls
  • Do not run drains if sewage is involved
  • Keep any receipts for repairs and cleanup
  • Ask who authorizes plumbing work before calling anyone

When a drain problem is bigger than your unit

If several units report slow or gurgling drains on the same floor, the cause is likely in a shared line and not in your fixture. A camera pass from a cleanout shows where the blockage sits. Our sewer inspection page explains what that involves, and drain cleaning covers the clearing itself.

Midvale says lateral maintenance typically falls to the property owner, not the city. In an association, that owner is usually the association, so the board or manager should be the one to authorize shared-line work.
