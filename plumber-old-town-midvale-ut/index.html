The area

What Old Town and Main Street mean in Midvale

Midvale became a city in 1909, and Main Street was its central commercial area. The city's redevelopment page says smelter offices, banks, restaurants and shops clustered around the smelter west of Main Street, and that the nearby Avenues neighborhood held homes built for industrial workers and their families.

No local source publishes a legal boundary for Old Town, so we describe it by its anchor, the Main Street corridor that the city promotes as the Midvale Main Arts and Culture District. The Salt Lake Tribune reported in October 2026 that most original buildings there date from the early 1900s to the 1950s.

A 1970s interchange at 7200 South and I-215 cut Main Street off from the rest of the valley, according to the same report. The street has been a redevelopment focus since, which is why you now see restored storefronts next to new construction.

Building types

Four plumbing setups you meet on and near Main Street

The district is not one kind of building, and the right first step depends on which one you own or rent.

Older single-family houses

The city's 2020 redevelopment report counted 181 single-family parcels and 13 multi-family parcels in the Main Street area. House age varies, so locate your main shutoff, sewer cleanout and water heater before something fails.

Storefronts and restaurants

Coffee shops, restaurants and venues put steady use on sinks, restroom fixtures and floor drains. In a leased space, the lease decides who pays for what, so read it before you call.

Mixed-use buildings

A 2023 regional planning summary describes Main Street projects with residential units above commercial space. In a shared building a leak can reach the unit below, so find the building shutoff early.

New townhomes and condos

The Tribune reports townhomes and condos being built on Main Street lots. Owners should check what the association covers, since supply lines, shared drains and in-unit fixtures often have different owners.

Private pipe or city pipe? A quick way to sort it out

Many calls on older streets come down to one question, and these four checks answer most of it.

  1. 1

    Count the fixtures

    One slow sink points to a branch line. Several slow fixtures point to the main line.

  2. 2

    Look outside

    Find the sewer cleanout, the water meter and the shutoff. Standing water near any of them matters.

  3. 3

    Call the city for street problems

    Water in the street or no water to the block belongs with Midvale Public Works at 801-567-7235.

  4. 4

    Call a plumber for your side

    Anything between the meter and your fixtures, or between your walls and the sewer main, is usually yours.

If a cable clears a clog but it keeps returning, a camera pass can show why. Our drain cleaning and sewer inspection pages explain both.

Sewer work on Main Street and what it means for your line

In June 2026 the city closed Main Street at Wasatch Street from June 22 to July 1 for what it called an essential sewer system repair, with local access kept open. That was public work in the street, which is a different pipe from the lateral that connects a building to the main.

Midvale says lateral maintenance and ownership typically falls to the property owner, not the city. If several fixtures back up at once, or a floor drain gurgles when a toilet flushes, the cause may be in your lateral or in the main, and a camera shows which.

Buying or renovating near Main Street? Check the plumbing first

The city says remodeling, additions and water heater replacement require a building permit and inspections, and only the contractor on record schedules inspections. Ask Midvale Building and Safety at 801-567-7200 before work starts, because we cannot confirm permit rules for every job.

Before you buy an older building, a camera look at the sewer lateral shows its condition without digging. Pipe age and material differ from lot to lot, and no public source maps them, so an inspection beats an assumption.
