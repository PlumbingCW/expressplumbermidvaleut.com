What we install

Fixtures and lines we put in

Most installation calls fall into a few categories.

Faucets, sinks and vanities

New supply lines and shutoffs, correct trap height and a leak test under load.

Toilets and bidet seats

A new wax or rubber seal, a level flange and a supply that matches the tank inlet.

Showers and tubs

Valve rough-in, drain connections and a pressure test before tile covers the plumbing.

New water and drain lines

Added bathrooms, laundry rooms or wet bars need supply, drain and vent that work together.

Hose bibs and appliances

Frost-resistant outdoor faucets, dishwasher and ice maker lines, and disposals.

Basement finishing

Rough-in for a basement bath, with a sewage ejector when the drain sits below the main line.

Permit questions

Permits and inspections in Midvale

Midvale City says new construction, additions, basement finishes and remodeling require a building permit and building inspections. Permits are submitted online through Midvale Building and Safety at City Hall, 7505 S Holden Street, and the office is open Monday through Thursday.

Whether a simple fixture swap needs one is a question for the city, so ask Midvale Building and Safety before you start. Inspection requests go in at least 24 hours ahead, and only the contractor on record can schedule them. For new lines in a finished basement, plan the permit into your timeline.

Remodel plumbing: before the drywall or after

Timing matters more than most owners expect.

Plumbing after walls close

  • Fewer routes for new pipe
  • Open and patch drywall later
  • Higher risk of damaging finished tile
  • Harder to inspect hidden work

Plumbing before walls close

  • Freedom to run supply, drain and vent
  • Pressure test and inspection while open
  • Rough-in set at the right height
  • Lower patching costs
Water pressure and fixtures

What Midvale's water means for new fixtures

Midvale City requires water-saving fixtures in new construction under the International Plumbing Code. If you are shopping for new faucets, showerheads or toilets, look for flow ratings on the box rather than assuming older numbers apply.

Some east-side homes use water purchased from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, and hard water can leave scale on fixtures faster. If aerators clog often, a water softener conversation may be worth having before you buy expensive finishes.
