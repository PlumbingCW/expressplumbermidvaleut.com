Signs your pipes may need replacing

One leak is a repair. A pattern is a decision.

Brown or orange water

Rust flakes from the inside of old steel pipe. It often shows first after the water has sat unused overnight.

Low pressure at several fixtures

Galvanized pipe corrodes inward, so a pipe that once carried a full flow slowly narrows to the width of a pencil.

Repeat pinhole leaks

Each patch moves the stress to the next weak spot. Three leaks in a year usually means the rest of the line is similar.

Gray plastic pipe

Polybutylene was installed in US homes from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It can fail at fittings without warning.

Copper or PEX: the honest comparison

Both are good choices for a repipe. The right one depends on your house, your water and your budget.

Neither is better in every house. We will explain why we lean one way for yours.

Old pipes outside the house too

Midvale City's own water plan describes many distribution pipes as old, undersized and possibly leaking. The city replaces galvanized service laterals with copper or poly when they leak and during pipeline projects, aiming for about 1,000 feet of pipeline per year.

The practical point is the line from the meter to your house is generally the property owner's concern. If your inside pipes are galvanized and the lateral is too, repiping one without checking the other can leave a weak link. Call the city's Public Utilities office about the status of your service line before you finalize a plan, and see our water line repair page if the problem is outside the house.

How a repipe goes

Repiping is disruptive, but a good plan keeps water off for as short a time as possible.

  1. 1

    Walk and plan

    We map every fixture, the main shutoff and the water heater, then pick pipe routes.

  2. 2

    Open access

    Small drywall cuts or attic and crawlspace access let us run new lines.

  3. 3

    Run new lines

    New pipe goes in beside the old, with fittings kept out of hard-to-reach spots.

  4. 4

    Connect and test

    Fixtures are reconnected and the system is pressure tested before closing walls.

  5. 5

    Patch and permit

    Access holes are closed up, and we ask Midvale Building and Safety about permit and inspection needs.

Patching leaks or repiping

Both are reasonable at different stages. This is how to tell where you are.

Keep patching

  • One leak in otherwise sound pipe
  • Pipe is copper or newer plastic in good shape
  • Pressure and water color are normal
  • You plan to sell soon and the pipes are not failing

Plan a repipe

  • Galvanized or polybutylene throughout
  • Three or more leaks in a year
  • Rusty water and falling pressure
  • Planning a remodel that will open walls anyway

Where this guidance comes from

New piping follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the pipe maker's guidelines for supports and joints. Whole-house repipes are permit work in many cities, so ask Midvale Building and Safety.
