When a sewer problem is an emergency

A slow drain can wait for a day. A sewer line that has failed behaves differently and gets worse with each use of water.

Call right away if you see any of these:

  • Sewage or dirty water coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain
  • Several fixtures backing up at once, or a toilet gurgling when the washer drains
  • A foul smell in the yard or basement with a wet or sunken patch of lawn
  • A cleanout cap with water standing at or above it
  • Raw sewage inside the house, which carries bacteria and calls for protective cleanup

Two repair paths

Trenchless pipe lining or digging it up

Many homeowners ask about trenchless lining, the method where a resin-soaked liner is inflated inside the old pipe and cured in place. It is a real option and it is not always the right one.

Pipe lining (trenchless)

  • Needs little or no digging, so the lawn and driveway stay
  • Works in pipe that is cracked, root-invaded or has leaking joints
  • Needs a line that is still roughly round and open
  • Does not fix a collapsed section or a major low spot

Open-trench replacement

  • Replaces the failed pipe with new material
  • Fixes collapsed pipe and belly sections
  • Can correct the slope
  • Means more digging, surface repair and time

A camera inspection decides which one fits. We will tell you plainly if lining will not hold, rather than lining a pipe that needs replacing.

Pipe types

Cast iron, clay and other sewer pipe

The age of the house often tells us the pipe material, and the material tells us the likely failure.

Cast iron

Common in older drain lines. It rusts from the inside out, scales, and eventually thins until it cracks or sags. A cast iron section that is flaking can be lined or replaced, depending on the camera.

Clay tile

Older laterals often use short clay sections with many joints. Roots find the joints, and ground movement can offset them.

Orangeburg

A tar-and-fiber pipe used in some mid-century homes. It softens, deforms and collapses, so lining rarely works and replacement is typical.

PVC and ABS

Plastic pipe from later decades. It resists roots and corrosion but can crack from impact or settle at poorly compacted trenches.

Midvale ownership

Who owns the sewer line, and which district serves you

Midvale says maintenance and ownership of the sewer lateral typically falls to the property owner. That is the pipe between your house and the main, so a break there is yours to fix.

The main in the street belongs to whichever of four sewer districts serves your block: Midvale City Public Works, Cottonwood Improvement District, Midvalley Improvement District or Sandy Suburban Improvement District. Midvale City collects wastewater for only about one-third of the city, so do not assume the city is the one to call. Tell us your address and we will help sort out where your lateral meets the main.

Sewer or septic, and what we do not do

Most Midvale addresses are on a sewer, but if your property has a septic tank, tell us when you call. Septic pumping and drain field work are a different trade, and we will say plainly whether the problem is in our scope.

We do not fix the street main. If the camera shows the blockage or break is past the property line, we document it and point you to the district that serves your street. A clog in the main in the road can look exactly like a clog in your line, so this step saves a repair bill you did not need.
