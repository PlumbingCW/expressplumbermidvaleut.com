Tankless Water Heater Installation, Repair and Flushing in Midvale, UT

A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so it never runs out the way a tank can. It also asks more of your house. Expect a bigger gas line, special vent pipe, and a flush once a year or so to keep the heat exchanger clear of scale.

Why a tankless heater needs flushing in Midvale

Minerals in the water coat the inside of the heat exchanger as the unit runs. Scale narrows the passages, the heater works harder, and you start seeing error codes, temperature swings or a unit that shuts off mid-shower.

The Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District reported total hardness in its 2025 water quality report at an average of 182.4 mg/L, with samples from 16 up to 381. The USGS calls water above 180 mg/L very hard, so an average of 182.4 sits just over that line and individual taps can land on either side.

West-side Midvale is served from city-owned wells, and the city's pages give no hardness figure. Ask Midvale Public Utilities for the annual water quality report, or have your tap tested. If your water is on the hard end, yearly flushing is not optional, and a softener upstream is worth pricing out. See water softener installation for how that works.

How a tankless flush works

Most units have two service valves that let us circulate descaler through the heat exchanger.

  1. 1

    Isolate the unit

    We turn off gas and power, close the water, and open the service valves.

  2. 2

    Circulate descaler

    A small pump moves a mild acid solution through the exchanger, usually for 45 minutes to an hour.

  3. 3

    Rinse and clean filters

    We flush with clean water and wash the inlet screen, which catches grit.

  4. 4

    Restart and check

    We light the burner, check flow and temperature, and read any stored error codes.

Many manufacturers call for a flush about once a year in hard water. If yours has never been flushed, call sooner, since heavy scale may need more than one pass.

Common tankless problems we are asked about

Callers usually describe the symptom, not the part. These are the patterns behind the most common ones.

Error code and no hot water

Codes point to ignition, flame, vent or flow faults. Some clear with a flush, and others need a part such as a sensor or igniter.

Cold sandwich

A burst of cold water between hot ones happens when flow drops and restarts. Scale or low flow settings are the usual cause.

Leak at the unit

Check the service valves and the condensate line first. A leak inside the case needs a plumber, and the unit should be shut off.

Not heating enough

Scale, a clogged inlet screen or too many fixtures running at once can all cut output.

Flush it yourself or call a plumber

A capable homeowner can flush a tankless heater with the right kit. Know the limits first.

DIY flush

  • Needs service valves already installed
  • Needs a small pump, hoses and descaler
  • Easy to miss gas and power shutoff steps
  • Does not diagnose why it is failing

Plumber flush

  • Valves added if your unit lacks them
  • Checks combustion, flow and codes
  • Cleans the inlet screen and condensate trap
  • Spots vent or gas problems early

What affects tankless pricing

Tankless work varies more than tank work because the house has to support the unit.

Unit capacityLarger households need a higher flow rating, which costs more.
Gas line upgradeA bigger gas pipe means more material and labor.
Vent routeA long run or a roof penetration adds time.
Existing service valvesFlushing is faster when valves are already installed.
Condition of the unitHeavy scale or failed parts change a flush into a repair.

If you smell gas near the heater

Leave the building right away and leave doors open if it is safe. From outside, call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911.

Do not switch lights, use a phone indoors or try to find the leak. Once the utility clears the site, we can inspect the heater and its connections.

  • Sulfur or rotten egg smell
  • Hissing near the gas line
  • Dizziness or headache indoors
