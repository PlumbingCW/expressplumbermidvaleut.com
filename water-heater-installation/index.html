Choosing a type

Gas, electric or heat pump: which water heater fits?

Most Midvale homes already have a fuel hookup, and switching fuels adds work. Start with what you have, then ask whether a change makes sense.

Tankless installs have their own page. See tankless water heater installation for sizing and venting.
TypeBest fitWhat it needsWatch out for
Gas tankHomes with a gas line and a vent already in placeA flue to the roof, a gas shutoff and a sediment trapCombustion air in tight closets
Electric tankHomes with no gas, or a condo or apartment unitA dedicated 240 volt circuit, usually 30 ampsSlower recovery, so a bigger tank helps

TanklessHouseholds that want endless hot water and have space on the wallLarger gas line and special venting for gas modelsNeeds yearly flushing in hard water

A heat pump unit uses roughly a third of the electricity of a standard electric tank, because it moves heat instead of making it. The trade is a higher purchase price and a larger body, often 7 feet tall.

Permit rule

Midvale requires a permit and inspection for water heaters

Midvale City states that water heater replacement requires a building permit and building inspections. Permits are submitted online through Midvale Building and Safety at City Hall, 7505 S Holden Street.

Inspections are requested online at least 24 hours ahead, and only the contractor on record can schedule them. That is a real reason to hire a plumber for the swap, since an unpermitted heater can cause trouble when you sell or file an insurance claim. We pull the permit and meet the inspector, so you do not have to chase the schedule.

What happens on install day

A standard tank swap usually fills most of a day. Gas conversions or relocations take longer.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    We close the water and fuel, then drain the old tank to a floor drain or the yard.

  2. 2

    Remove and prepare

    The old unit goes out, and we check the drain pan, the flue and the shutoff valves.

  3. 3

    Set and connect

    New flex connectors, a relief valve and discharge pipe go in, plus an expansion tank where needed.

  4. 4

    Fill and test

    We purge air, check every joint for drips, then light the burner or power the elements.

  5. 5

    Inspection and handoff

    The city inspection is scheduled, and we show you the shutoff and the temperature dial.

If the old tank is already leaking onto the floor, read about water heater repair first. Sometimes a failed valve is the whole story and the tank has years left.

Parts that should change with the heater

A good install replaces the small parts that get reused too often.

Temperature and pressure relief valve

This safety valve opens if the tank overheats or overpressures. Its discharge pipe must run downhill to a safe spot, never capped.

Water and gas connectors

Old flex lines crack and corrode at the ends. Replacing them is cheap and avoids a slow leak behind the tank.

Expansion tank

On a closed system with a pressure regulator or backflow device, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small tank takes up that pressure and protects fittings.

Drain pan and shutoff valve

A pan with a drain line catches a failed tank in a closet or attic. A working shutoff above the heater lets you stop water fast.

Sizing check before you buy

A tank that is too small runs out. One that is too large wastes fuel every day.

  • Count people in the house. Two to three people usually need a 40 gallon tank, and four or five often need 50 to 60 gallons.
  • Note the peak morning use, such as two showers and a dishwasher at once.
  • Measure the space, including height under a ceiling, a closet door width and clearance for the vent.
  • Check whether the old unit is gas or electric and where its flue or circuit runs.
  • Look at the data plate on your current heater for its first-hour rating and age.

Most tank heaters last 8 to 12 years. If yours is near that age and starting to leak, replacing it on your own schedule is cheaper than doing it after a flood.

What affects the cost of a new water heater

Two homes with the same tank size can get different totals. These are the usual reasons.

Type and capacityA heat pump unit costs more up front than a standard gas or electric tank.
Venting and gas lineA new flue path or a larger gas pipe adds parts and labor.
LocationAn attic or tight closet takes more time than a garage.
Code itemsExpansion tank, pan, seismic or strapping needs and permit fees add to the total.
Haul-awayRemoving and disposing of the old tank is part of the job.

Homeowners insurance may cover a sudden tank burst and the water damage, but it rarely pays to replace a tank that simply wore out. Photograph the old data plate and keep the invoice.

If gas is involved

If you smell sulfur or rotten eggs near a gas heater, leave the house with doors open, then call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors.
