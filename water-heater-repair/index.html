When a water heater problem is an emergency

A water heater stores 40 to 50 gallons under pressure, and most of that water is hot enough to scald. Treat the problem as urgent if any of these are happening:

  • Water running from the bottom of the tank, not just a drip from a fitting
  • Hot water or steam coming out of the pipe attached to the relief valve
  • A rumbling, banging or popping sound from the tank
  • Scorch marks, soot or a burning smell around a gas unit
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater turns on
Gas first

If you smell gas, leave first

A gas water heater with a sulfur or rotten-egg smell nearby is not a repair call yet. Leave the building with the door open if it is safe, and do not touch light switches or phones inside.

From outside, call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911. Once the utility says the house is safe, call us and we will look at the heater.

Leaks

Why a water heater leaks, and what each leak means

Leaks are the most common reason people call about a water heater. Where the water comes from decides whether it is a quick fix or a replacement.

Loose fittings on top

Water on top of the tank often comes from an inlet or outlet connection. Tightening or replacing a nipple is a short repair.

Relief valve discharge

A wet floor under the discharge pipe can mean the valve is worn, or that pressure or temperature is climbing too high.

Drain valve drip

The plastic or brass valve near the bottom can weep. Replacing it takes less time than draining the tank to look.

Tank body rust-through

Water seeping from the steel shell means the inner tank has corroded. No patch holds, and the heater needs replacing.

If the tank itself is leaking, we will say so plainly and talk through water heater replacement instead of selling a repair that will not last.

Heater types

Gas, electric and hybrid heater repair

The repairs differ by fuel and by design, so tell us what is in your utility closet when you call.

Gas heaters

Common repairs include thermocouples, pilot assemblies, gas control valves and flue draft problems. Burner and venting work stays with a licensed plumber because a bad flue can push exhaust into the house.

Electric heaters

Most no-hot-water calls come down to a heating element or thermostat. The tank has two of each, and a dead lower element often leaves you with a short supply of hot water.

Hybrid (heat pump) heaters

These combine a heat pump on top with backup elements. We handle the electrical side, condensate drain, air filter, relief valve and plumbing connections. Tell us the model when you call, since sealed refrigerant work can be a specialist job.

Older tank units

A heater past roughly 10 to 12 years often costs more to nurse along than to replace. We will say if a repair does not make sense.

Safety

Relief valve and pressure safety

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the part that keeps a stuck thermostat from turning the tank into a pressure vessel. It opens when temperature or pressure goes too high, and a pipe on its side should run toward the floor so scalding water goes somewhere safe.

Never cap that pipe, plug it, or screw a fitting into the valve to stop a drip. A valve that drips steadily is telling you something, such as thermal expansion in a closed system or a worn seat, and we will find out which one before replacing anything.

Midvale water

How Midvale water wears on a tank

East-side Midvale gets water purchased from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, whose 2025 report lists average total hardness of 182.4 mg/L, about 10.7 grains per gallon. That is on the very hard side by USGS classes, and it means scale builds on the bottom of a tank and coats electric elements, so a flush now and then is worth asking about.

West-side homes are fed by city wells, and the city does not publish a hardness figure. Ask Midvale Public Utilities for the annual drinking water report if you want to know what your tank is working against.

Repair it or replace it?

The answer depends on the age of the tank and where the failure is.

Repair makes sense when

  • The heater is under about 10 years old
  • A part like an element, thermostat or valve failed
  • The tank is dry and the shell is sound
  • You want to buy time for a planned upgrade

Replace when

  • The tank shell is leaking
  • Rust-colored water keeps returning
  • The heater is past 12 years and failing again
  • Repair parts would cost close to a new unit
