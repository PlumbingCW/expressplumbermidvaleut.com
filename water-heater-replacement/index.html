Signs your water heater needs replacing, not repair

A repair makes sense for a failed part on a healthy tank. These signs point the other way:

  • Water leaking from the tank body, not from a fitting
  • Hot water that comes out rusty or brown after sitting
  • A unit that is 10 to 12 years old and has failed more than once
  • Heavy sediment noise that returns right after a flush
  • A cracked flue or burner chamber on a gas unit

If you are unsure, a short look can tell the two apart. Our water heater repair page lists symptoms that a repair can fix.

What to do while you wait for a replacement

A failed tank can leak, scald or lose gas safety features, so take these steps first.

  • Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater.
  • Switch off the electric breaker or set a gas heater's control to off.
  • If you smell gas, leave the home and call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 before anyone else.
  • Keep people away from standing water near outlets.

When a failed water heater is an emergency

A slow drip can wait for a day. These cannot:

  • A steady stream or pool of water under the tank
  • Water near the electrical panel or on a basement floor with outlets
  • No hot water with a newborn, an older relative or anyone medically fragile in the house
  • A gas smell near the unit. Leave and call Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 first, then call us

Midvale permit

Permit and inspection for water heater replacement in Midvale

Midvale Building and Safety lists water heater replacement among the work that needs a building permit and building inspections. That is not optional paperwork. An inspector checks things like the relief valve discharge, venting and connections that a homeowner cannot judge by looking at hot water.

Permits are submitted online. Inspections are requested online at least 24 hours ahead on Monday through Thursday, and only the contractor on record can schedule them, so settle who that is before work starts. The office is at City Hall, 7505 S Holden Street, second floor, open Monday through Thursday 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. and closed Friday to Sunday, so an emergency swap at midnight may be inspected a few days later.

Old to new

What changes when you replace an older tank

New units come with different dimensions and connections than a heater from the 1990s. A new tank may need fresh water connectors, a dielectric union, a new relief valve and a properly routed discharge pipe.

Older homes often have shutoff valves that have not moved in years and can stick or break when turned. If the valve above your heater will not close, tell us on the phone so we can plan how to isolate the tank.

Water quality

Midvale water and your new tank

Hard water shortens the life of any heater, and Midvale has two different supplies.

East side, JVWCD water

Jordan Valley Water's 2025 report shows average hardness of 182.4 mg/L, near 10.7 grains per gallon. Plan on flushing the tank, and ask whether a softener makes sense.

West side, city wells

Midvale draws from three wells, and the city publishes no hardness figure. Test your tap or ask Public Utilities for the annual water report.

Thermal expansion

If your system has a backflow device or closed valve at the meter, heating water has nowhere to go. An expansion tank handles that.

Anode rods

Replacing the rod when it is consumed slows tank corrosion, but only if someone checks it.

A water softener installation is one way to reduce scale on elements and tank walls.
