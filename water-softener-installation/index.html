The Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District reported these figures in its 2025 water quality report. The average sits just over the line the USGS uses for very hard water, and the range is wide, so two taps can differ a lot.

Which Midvale water do you have?

East-side Midvale water is purchased from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, so the JVWCD report applies. West-side water comes from city-owned wells, and the city's pages give no hardness figure.

If you live on the west side, ask Midvale Public Utilities for the annual water quality report at 801-567-7239. Better still, test your own tap with a hardness strip or a lab test, because pipe, location and season all change the result. Do that before buying any equipment.

The test-your-tap approach

A softener sized to the wrong number either wastes salt or leaves you with hard water.

  1. 1

    Test a cold tap

    Use an indoor kitchen or bath tap and run it a minute first. Record the result in mg/L or grains.

  2. 2

    Count your household

    People, showers and laundry loads set daily water use, which sizes the unit.

  3. 3

    Calculate capacity

    Hardness in grains times gallons used daily gives grains per day to remove.

  4. 4

    Choose and place

    Pick a unit and a location near the main line, a drain, an outlet and a bypass.

We can walk through this with you by phone. If the test comes back soft or only moderately hard, you may not need a softener at all.

Softener, conditioner or filter: what each does

The names get mixed up in advertising. They are not interchangeable.

Hardness is only one water issue. Taste or odor problems may need a different fix.
SystemWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerRemoves calcium and magnesium by ion exchange, which stops scaleDoes not filter chlorine or remove sediment. Adds a little sodium
Salt-free conditionerChanges how scale forms so it clings lessDoes not remove hardness. Results vary
Whole-house sediment filterCatches sand and gritDoes not soften
Carbon filterReduces chlorine taste and odorDoes not soften
Reverse osmosis at the sinkCleans drinking water at one tapDoes not protect the water heater

What proper softener installation includes

A good hookup is more than connecting two hoses.

Bypass valve

Lets you isolate the softener for service or when you want unsoftened water.

Drain connection

The unit flushes brine to a drain. An air gap keeps wastewater from siphoning back into the unit.

Hard water tap

Keep an outdoor hose bib on unsoftened water so you do not waste salt on the lawn.

Electrical outlet

The control head needs power, with the cord kept off the floor in a damp basement.

Pressure and flow check

We test pressure before and after to confirm the unit is not choking flow.

Softener repair and relocation

Many calls are about a softener that stopped working, not a new one. Common causes are a stuck valve, a brine tank bridge, a clogged injector, a broken drain line or a power fault. Some units are cheaper to replace once the control head or tank fails, and we will tell you which side of that line yours falls on.

If you are moving a softener during a remodel, protect the drain line and the bypass during construction. A cut regeneration line or a missing drain turns a good unit into a flood.
